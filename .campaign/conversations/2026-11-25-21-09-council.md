**🐻 Bear:**

1. **"Dry" is the main brief, and topicality is how you deliver it.** New Zealand comedy at its best is deadpan officialdom: understatement, signs that say too little, officials who are calm about absurd things. The game's voice should be institutional. Signage, radio calls and HUD alerts deliver the joke, never a wacky character mugging at the camera.
2. **The news gives you strong material right now:**
   - a knife-edge election (7 Nov) with special votes due on the 27th;
   - a 5.7 quake near Arthur's Pass, with aftershocks expected for weeks;
   - petrol driving up living costs;
   - record numbers of Kiwis leaving for Australia.
3. **Enemies should be animals or objects, never people.** That's both funnier and fairer. New Zealand is unusually well supplied with cheeky wildlife and introduced pests that everyone already has opinions about.
4. **Pick the idea where the topical hook is the setting, not the punchline.** A joke about a specific headline is dated by next week. An aftershock-closed road or an emptying town still works in a year.

**🐱 Cat:**

1. **Shooting native species is a real risk.** Kea are endangered and much loved. If you shoot one, the audience turns on you. Any native bird must be repelled without being harmed, and it should come back offended. Introduced pests like possums, rabbits and magpies are fair game in NZ, but keep it bloodless anyway; it suits a dry tone better.
2. **Steer clear of real people and organisations.** No real politicians, parties, broadcasters (RNZ is in the middle of a job-cuts dispute), retail brands or government agencies by name or logo. Fictional stand-ins like "the Department" or "the Returning Office" are funnier anyway.
3. **Don't touch the hard stories:** the child-poverty reports, the police custody death and the school-punishment story. The quake is fine as a backdrop if nobody is shown as a victim. Christchurch and Kaikōura are still raw.
4. **Election humour goes stale quickly and risks looking partisan.** Final results land in two days and coalition talks follow. A game takes hours to build and weeks to share, so the news may have moved on by launch.
5. **Nothing in the pipeline judges comedy.** The skill's critic sees only images and scores lighting and materials. It can't tell a good joke on a sign from a bad one.
